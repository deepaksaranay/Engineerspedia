About Engineerspedia

Engineering knowledge, without the jargon.

Most engineering writing is aimed at people who already understand it. Textbooks assume a semester of prerequisites; documentation assumes you know why you're reading it; and the explanations that are accessible are often accessible because they're quietly wrong.

Engineerspedia sits in the gap. Every article here explains one idea properly — with the real mechanism, not a comforting analogy that falls apart under a follow-up question — in language that doesn't require a degree to follow.

What we write about

Six disciplines, one article at a time:

    How the articles are written

    • One idea per article. If a topic needs two, it becomes two articles.
    • The mechanism, not the mnemonic. We'd rather spend a paragraph on why something is true than give you a rule to memorise.
    • Concrete before abstract. A worked example, a real failure, a number you can sanity-check — then the general principle.
    • Honest about limits. Where a simplification breaks down, we say so instead of hoping you don't notice.
    • No paywall. Everything is free, and there are no ads.

    Who it's for

    Students who want the intuition their lectures skipped. Engineers reading outside their own discipline. Writers, product managers and founders working alongside engineers. And anyone who has ever looked at a bridge, a jet engine or a codebase and wondered how it actually holds together.

    Corrections

    If something here is wrong, we want to know — precision is the entire point. Send a note through the contact page and it'll get fixed, with the correction noted on the article.
